Intelligent Relations API

Version v1 Base URL https://{host}/api/v1 Last updated 18 August 2026

This document is written to be sufficient on its own. If you find something ambiguous, that is a fault in the document — tell us and we will fix it.

1Core concepts

1.1 The object model

Account ──────────── your organisation and its brand profile
  │
  ├── Company profile     name, website, positioning, themes, keywords
  ├── Spokesperson        a named person available for quotes and interviews
  ├── Competitor          a tracked competitor
  ├── Analysis            brand analysis: positioning, messaging, PR plan
  ├── Sender              a connected sending mailbox
  ├── Campaign draft      a prepared, not-yet-launched campaign
  ├── Campaign            a launched outreach effort
  ├── Email               an individual message, outbound or inbound
  ├── Interaction         a classified reply outcome
  └── Wire release        a press release for distribution

Journalist ─────────── a media contact. Shared, read-only.
Publication ────────── an outlet
Podcast ────────────── a podcast outlet
Article ────────────── a published story

An account holds one brand profile. Company name, website, positioning, messaging, themes, spokespeople and the PR plan are all attributes of the account. An API key belongs to exactly one account, and therefore to exactly one brand.

If you manage several brands — your own products, or client brands as an agency — each brand needs its own account and its own API key. Design for this from the start: hold a key per brand and select the right one per request, rather than assuming one credential covers your whole portfolio. See §2.1.

1.2 How the pieces fit together

The typical flow, and the order that matters:

  company profile
        │
        ▼
    analysis  ────────────▶  PR plan / campaign ideas
        │
        ▼
  journalist discovery  ◀──── a pitch or an angle
        │
        ▼
  pitch generation  ──▶  subject line  ──▶  personalisation
        │
        ▼
  campaign draft  ──▶  human review  ──▶  launch
        │
        ▼
     replies  ──▶  interactions  ──▶  your response

Two properties of this flow shape your integration:

The company profile drives everything downstream. Journalist relevance and pitch quality both derive from it. A sparse profile produces broad matches and generic copy — this shows up as weak reply rates rather than as errors. §6.2 covers what to populate.

Pitch generation and campaign creation are separate steps. No single call produces a finished campaign. You generate copy, review it, then pass it into a campaign. This is deliberate: it puts a human approval point in front of anything that sends email to a journalist.

1.4 What the API does not do

Stated up front so you do not design against absent capability.

  • No account or brand creation. Accounts are provisioned by us. See §2.1.
  • No journalist email addresses. You target journalists by ID and we deliver. See §10.2.
  • No relevance scores or ranking explanations. Results are ordered; the ordering is not quantified. See §9.6.
  • No journalist record editing. The dataset is shared across all customers.
  • No single-call campaign generation. Copy generation and campaign creation are separate. See §1.2.

2Getting started

2.1 Getting an account and an API key

Accounts are provisioned by us as part of commercial onboarding, one per brand. You receive an API key for each through a secure channel.

There is no public signup endpoint. Account creation involves contract acceptance and billing enrolment, so it is handled by your account manager rather than by an API call.

If you need many brands, tell us the expected number and growth rate during onboarding so we can provision in bulk rather than one at a time. See §12 for planned self-serve provisioning.

2.2 Your first call

Confirm the key works and see which account it maps to:

HTTP
GET /api/v1/me
Authorization: Bearer 8f2c47a19e3d5b06c81f92e3b7d54c4f
JSON
{
  "data": {
    "account_id": 4821,
    "account_name": "Northwind Robotics"
  }
}

This establishes the most important rule in the API: the account is determined by your API key, never by the request body. You never send an account identifier. Cache account_id for your own logging and correlation, and never accept one as input from your end users.

2.3 Your first real call

Fetch journalists relevant to your brand:

HTTP
POST /api/v1/journalists/top
Authorization: Bearer 8f2c47a19e3d5b06c81f92e3b7d54c4f
Content-Type: application/json

{ "include_articles": true }

If this returns few results or loose matches, the company profile needs enriching before anything else — start at §6.2.

3Authentication and scopes

3.1 Scheme

Bearer token over HTTPS on every request.

Authorization: Bearer 8f2c47a19e3d5b06c81f92e3b7d54c4f

Tokens are opaque strings — do not parse them. Treat a token as a full-account credential: store it in a secret manager, never in source control, never in client-side code, and never in a URL query string.

If a token is exposed, contact us immediately for rotation (§13). Self-serve rotation is on the roadmap (§12).

3.2 One key, one account

Each key is bound to a single account. There is no cross-account access and no way to widen a key's reach.

For a multi-brand integration, hold one key per brand and resolve the right key before each request. Confirm a key's account with GET /me when you first store it, and record the mapping on your side.

3.3 Scopes

Keys carry scopes limiting what they can do. Scopes take the form read:<resource> or write:<resource>, with read:all and write:all as wildcards.

Resourceread:write:
accountCompany profile, spokespeople, competitors, analysisUpdate profile, spokespeople, competitors
journalistsSearch, retrieve, top and relevant journalists—
publicationsPublications and podcasts—
articlesStory search, trending, relevant—
campaignsCampaigns, drafts, campaign ideasCreate drafts, launch, pause, delete
pitches—Generate pitch, subject line, follow-up, personalisation
emailsSent, drafted, scheduled email and repliesSend, reply
interactionsReply history—
listsRetrieve and search listsSave lists
wireRetrieve releasesSubmit releases
sendersMailbox statusConnect, disconnect

Request the narrowest set that does the job. write:emails and write:campaigns are the two that cause real email to be sent — a service that only reads analytics should never hold them. If your architecture separates campaign preparation from dispatch, use separate keys with separate scopes so a bug in the preparation path cannot send.

A call missing a required scope returns 403 naming the scope needed. It does not return 404.

4Conventions

4.1 Base URL and versioning

https://{host}/api/v1

{host} is your assigned environment hostname, supplied with your key. The major version is in the path.

What may change without notice inside v1 — your client must tolerate all of these:

  • New fields in responses
  • New values in enums
  • New endpoints
  • New optional request fields

What will not change inside v1:

  • Removing or renaming a field
  • Changing a field's type or nullability
  • Removing an enum value
  • Changing the meaning of an existing parameter
  • Changing an error code for an existing condition

Breaking changes ship as v2, announced at least 90 days ahead, with both versions live for at least 12 months.

Two client requirements follow from this. Parse JSON leniently and ignore unknown fields — if your HTTP client or code generator errors on unrecognised properties, disable that before going live. And give every enum branch a default case.

4.2 Field naming

snake_case in all request and response bodies. Headers use standard Kebab-Case.

4.3 Identifiers

Identifiers are integers, unique within their type.

IdentifierScope
account_idDerived from your key. You never send it.
journalist_id, publication_id, podcast_id, article_idGlobal
campaign_id, draft_id, email_id, interaction_id, release_id, sender_idYour account

Account-scoped identifiers are only resolvable with a key for that account. An identifier from another account returns 404.

Wire releases additionally carry a guid UUID, used for de-duplication by downstream distribution.

4.4 Timestamps

ISO 8601 UTC with a Z suffix: 2026-08-18T09:15:47Z. Fields ending _at are timestamps; fields ending _date are calendar dates (2026-08-18) where time of day carries no meaning.

One deliberate exception. Wire release scheduling takes a wall-clock date, time and IANA timezone separately, because a press release goes out at 9am in a named city, not at a UTC instant. See §6.9.

4.5 Pagination

List endpoints are page-based.

ParameterTypeDefaultMaximum
pageinteger1—
page_sizeinteger10–50 by endpoint100
JSON
{
  "data": {
    "journalists": [],
    "total": 247,
    "page": 1,
    "page_size": 25
  }
}

total is a real count, so page count is ceil(total / page_size).

When paginating a set that changes underneath you — new stories arriving, new replies landing — page boundaries can shift, so a row may repeat or be skipped. For a stable pass over data, filter by an explicit date range rather than relying on page numbers alone, and de-duplicate on ID as you collect.

4.6 Response envelope

Success is always an object with a data key:

JSON
{ "data": { "campaign_id": 33914 } }

Never a bare array or scalar, so top-level fields can be added without breaking your parser.

Conventions that hold across every endpoint:

  • Stable keys. A given endpoint returns the same keys every time.
  • null versus []. Absent single values are null; empty collections are [], never null.
  • No polymorphism. A field never changes type by context.
  • Opt-in expansion. Lists return lean objects; related data is requested explicitly with flags like include_articles. Expansions cost latency — request them only when you will render them.
  • Display copy is marked. Fields marked Display copy are human-readable and safe to render. Everything else is for logic.

4.8 Rate limits

100 requests per minute per API key.

X-RateLimit-Limit: 100
X-RateLimit-Remaining: 73

On exceeding:

HTTP
HTTP/1.1 429 Too Many Requests
Retry-After: 27
JSON
{ "error": "Rate limit exceeded. Try again later.", "code": "rate_limited", "request_id": "req_6d1a93f7c4e08b25" }

Required client behaviour: honour Retry-After, then exponential backoff with jitter — sleep = random(0, min(60, 2^attempt)) seconds. Never retry in a tight loop.

Budget for generation, not for reads. Read endpoints are cheap and 100/minute is generous. The generation endpoints in §6.5 take seconds each, so in practice concurrency and latency constrain you well before the request limit does. Queue generation work on your side rather than firing it in parallel bursts.

5.1 What to expect

Every endpoint in §6 is synchronous — you make a request and get a result. Most are fast. The generation endpoints are slow enough that they shape your UI design, so plan for them explicitly.

5.2 Client requirements

Set your HTTP timeout to at least 90 seconds on the generation endpoints. A 30-second default will fail intermittently on /pitches/personalize.

Keep personalisation batches to 10–20 journalists. The endpoint accepts 50, but a full batch approaches 45 seconds — close to common proxy and gateway timeouts. Smaller batches are more reliable and let you show incremental progress.

Never call a generation endpoint in a user-blocking request path. Queue the work, return immediately, and update your UI when it completes. A user staring at a spinner for 30 seconds will assume it has broken.

Generate in the background, review in the foreground. The natural shape is: user requests a campaign → you queue generation → you notify them when copy is ready for review. That keeps the slowest work off the interactive path and puts the human approval step exactly where it belongs.

5.3 Brand analysis timing

Brand analysis is more substantial than a single generation call and takes 30 to 60 seconds end to end.

Analysis is currently initiated from the platform interface, and results are then available through GET /account/analysis (§6.3). API-initiated analysis with a pollable job resource is on the roadmap — see §12. Until it ships, plan for analysis being triggered outside your product, and read the results through the API.

Journalist discovery and pitch generation do not require a completed analysis. They work from the company profile alone, with somewhat less precision.

6Endpoint reference

6.1 Sending data to us

There are two ingest paths, with different shapes.

The company profile is durable brand data — read and updated over the life of the account, and the input to analysis, discovery and pitch generation. Covered in §6.2.

A wire release is a single time-bound announcement submitted for distribution. Covered in §6.9.

Free text, URLs and files are handled differently:

InputHowNotes
Free textInline in the request bodyUsed directly. Fastest path.
URLsInline as a field valueFetched server-side. Must be publicly reachable — no authentication, no cookies.

6.7 Campaigns

Lifecycle

A campaign has two phases, held as two separate records with different identifiers:

   campaign draft                    campaign

        │                                 │
   CANCELLED                              ▼

A campaign exists only once launched. Before that it is a draft with its own draft_id. A draft is not a campaign in a draft state.

7Notifications

Today the API is poll-based. Outbound webhooks are on the roadmap (§12).

Build your polling loop so it can be replaced by webhooks without restructuring: put event handling behind a function that takes an event, and have the poller call it. When webhooks arrive, you swap the source and keep the handler.

Recommended polling

WhatEndpointInterval
RepliesPOST /api/v1/interactions5 minutes

Mailbox healthGET /api/v1/senders15 minutes

Use overlapping windows and de-duplicate on ID. A query for exactly the last five minutes can drop an event that lands on the boundary. Query the last fifteen, and discard IDs you have already processed:

SQL
INSERT INTO processed_interactions (interaction_id, processed_at)
VALUES ($1, NOW())
ON CONFLICT (interaction_id) DO NOTHING;
-- proceed only if a row was inserted

Poll mailbox health even though it feels redundant. A mailbox whose authorisation lapses stops sending and stops receiving replies, so a silent failure looks exactly like a quiet week. See §8.4.

8Connecting a mailbox

8.1 Why this needs a browser

Outbound email is sent from a mailbox your user authorises. That authorisation is an interactive consent flow — a redirect to the mail provider, a human granting access, and a redirect back. A server-to-server integration cannot complete it alone.

We handle this with a hosted connect flow: you request a connect URL, redirect your user to it, and we manage the entire exchange. You never handle provider credentials, implement the consent flow, or store tokens.

9Journalist data: sourcing and verification

9.1 What the dataset is

Journalist data is maintained continuously rather than purchased as a static list. This section describes how it is built and kept current, so you can assess its quality and explain it to your users.

9.2 Where the data comes from

Published bylines are the primary source. Published articles are ingested continuously and attributed to their authors, so a journalist's record is grounded in work they actually published — and reflects what they cover now, not a role captured once. This is why include_articles returns real, dated, linkable work.

Public outlet and syndication data — mastheads, staff directories, editorial contact pages — establishes affiliation, role and desk, and is the main signal for detecting job changes.

Licensed and permissioned datasets from providers operating under their own compliance frameworks supplement and cross-check the above. They are never the sole basis for a contactable record.

Direct journalist updates take precedence over every other source. Journalists can correct their record, update their beats, or opt out entirely. A journalist who says they no longer cover a subject is believed immediately, over any inference from their published work.

9.3 Normalisation and de-duplication

Sources disagree constantly — the same person may appear as "Adaeze Nwankwo", "A. Nwankwo" and "Adaeze O. Nwankwo" across three outlets.

Normalisation standardises names, titles, outlets and locations. Name variants are tracked as aliases, so a byline under a former name still attributes correctly. Outlets resolve to canonical entities, so a publication and its regional editions are recognised as related.

De-duplication consolidates records for the same person, preserving the union of verified information. Ambiguous cases go to human review rather than being merged automatically, because an incorrect merge creates a record that misrepresents two real people.

9.4 Verification and freshness

Verification independently confirms three things: the person is still working as a journalist, the affiliation is current, and the contact route is deliverable. Deliverability is established without sending unsolicited mail.

Freshness is tracked per field, not per record — employment, title, location, bio and beat tags each carry their own last-updated state, so a stale job title does not make an otherwise-current record look fresh.

Verification cadence follows the likelihood of change: high-activity journalists, and those at outlets undergoing restructuring, are re-verified sooner.

last_verified_at appears on every journalist object:

JSON
{ "journalist_id": 88412, "name": "Adaeze Nwankwo", "last_verified_at": "2026-08-11T00:00:00Z", "is_contactable": true }

Read it as: as of this date, we confirmed this journalist is working, at this outlet, and reachable. It is not the last time any field changed, nor the last time they published. Date precision reflects that verification is a rolling process rather than an instant.

AgeInterpretation
Under 30 daysFresh
30–90 daysCurrent
90–180 daysAging — consider flagging for high-stakes outreach
Over 180 daysShould not appear as contactable. Tell us if you see one.

9.5 Retiring records

Records leave circulation through several routes, all reflected in is_contactable:

Delivery failure. A permanent failure marks a route unusable immediately and platform-wide, so a failure for one customer protects every other customer from the same dead address.

Failed verification. A record that cannot be re-verified is withheld, then retired. This is reversible — a journalist resurfacing at a new outlet is reinstated after fresh verification.

Career change. Leaving the profession retires the record. Moving outlets is an update, not a retirement.

Opt-out. A journalist requesting removal is retired immediately and permanently across the platform, and no customer can override it.

Outlet closure triggers accelerated re-verification rather than retirement, since most journalists move elsewhere.

Retired journalists remain retrievable by ID with is_contactable: false, so your historical campaign records stay renderable. Past campaigns never break; those journalists simply cannot be targeted again.

9.6 What we do not disclose

The sections above describe how the dataset is sourced, normalised, verified and retired. They deliberately do not describe how journalists are selected or ordered for a brand or a pitch.

No endpoint returns a relevance score, rank value, feature weight or match explanation. Boolean signals like is_high_response_rate replace the underlying figures.

This is intentional, for two reasons that also serve you. The selection methodology is the core of the product. And a published relevance model invites tuning inputs to inflate match counts — which degrades result quality for every customer, and means more poorly-targeted pitches reaching journalists, which is the fastest way to erode the goodwill the whole dataset depends on.

What you can rely on: ordering is meaningful, stable within a query, and derived from the profile and pitch you supply. Richer input produces better ordering — that is the lever available to you, and §6.2 explains how to use it.

10.2 Journalist personal data

Journalist data is personal data. We act as controller for it, and handle it accordingly.

Returned: name, current outlet, job title, beats and themes, location, public professional bio, published bylines, public professional social handles, your own engagement history, contactability and verification freshness. All of it either published professionally by the journalist or provided by them.

Never returned, under any scope: email address, phone number, and any other customer's engagement history.

Why addresses are withheld — this is the constraint most likely to shape your UI:

  1. It minimises how widely personal data is copied. Every additional copy is another exposure surface and another set of obligations.
  2. It makes opt-outs enforceable. Because sending happens through the platform, an unsubscribe is honoured universally and immediately. Exported addresses could not be.
  3. It preserves journalist trust. Journalists maintain their own records because their contact details are not redistributed. That cooperation is what keeps the data accurate — and accurate data is what makes your outreach land.
  4. It reduces your compliance burden. Journalist contact data never enters your database, logs, backups, or subject-access-request scope.

What this means practically. You cannot display an address, offer "copy email", or send through your own infrastructure. You can display full profiles, let users select journalists, edit copy, and send through §6.7 or §6.8 — which covers every workflow the product supports.

10.3 Generated content

Pitches, subject lines, personalisation lines, follow-ups and analysis text are generated, then post-processed and validated against a schema before return. Fields marked Display copy are safe to render.

Apply standard output encoding when rendering, as you would for any content that did not originate in your own code.

10.4 Your data

Data you submit — company profile, pitch copy, campaign configuration, wire releases — is processed on your behalf to deliver the service. We act as processor for it. Terms are in your data processing agreement; contact your account manager for a copy.

10.5 Unsubscribes and suppression

You own your suppression policy. You decide which journalists your brand does not contact, and filter your target lists accordingly.

Three conditions block sending permanently, regardless of your list:

ConditionWhy it cannot be overridden
UnsubscribeA legal requirement and a withdrawal of consent.
Permanent delivery failureThe address does not exist. Continued sending damages the sending domain's reputation, degrading deliverability for your campaigns and everyone else's.
Spam complaintThe strongest negative deliverability signal there is.

Each of these is unrecoverable if ignored: a journalist who receives mail after opting out does not un-receive it, and a blocked sending domain takes weeks to recover. Everything above that floor is your decision.

Affected journalists show is_contactable: false.

Handle UNSUBSCRIBED interactions immediately. A journalist may ask to be removed in reply text rather than through the unsubscribe link. When you see that interaction type, record the opt-out in your own system on the same pass — do not defer it to a later sync.

An unsubscribe footer is included in outbound campaign email as required by anti-spam law and by major mailbox providers. It is not removable.
